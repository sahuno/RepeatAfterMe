Lesson · Naming

Body and ends

A retrovirus-like copy is one piece of DNA, but the RepeatMasker table splits it into rows with different names: a body ending in "-int", and two ends called LTRs. Many ends have no body left.

Species

One copy, several rows

This is a real copy from the genome, drawn to scale. LTR stands for long terminal repeat: the same sequence sits at both ends.

Which ends go with which body

The table does not record which end belongs to which body, but their positions show it: for every body, the script ltr_structure.py looked for an LTR on the same strand right next to it.

How many ends still have a body

When the two ends of a copy recombine, the body between them is cut out and one end stays behind: a solo LTR. Recombination between two different proviruses can also leave one. The homepage animation shows it in the LTR mode.

Next to means within

Cases the rule gets wrong: a body broken by an insertion longer than 300 bp counts as two bodies with one end each; an LTR that later landed next to a body can be taken for its end; two proviruses in a row that share their middle LTR count as one with both ends and one with one end.

Older groups have more solo LTRs

Each dot is one group: a body name and its LTR names. Further right means older: its copies differ more from their reference sequence. Higher means a larger share of its LTRs are solo. Tap a dot to pick that group, or pick it in the Body menu above.

What this does to your counts

Reads counted for an LTR name come from every row with that name. Pick a name to see what those rows are.
