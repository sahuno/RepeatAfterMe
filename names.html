Lesson · Naming

Three names

Every repeat in the genome carries three names: class, family and subfamily. You count with the smallest one.

One row of the table

RepeatMasker is the program that finds repeats in a genome. Its table has one row per repeat fragment. This is the first transposon row on chromosome 1. Tap a name.

The table lists the smallest name first. Scroll left for the grey columns: positions and scores, not names.

Class, family, subfamily

Each row of boxes is one level of name. Box width is the share of the genome. Tap a box to look inside it. Tap a chromosome in the top row, or pick one from the Chromosome menu, to see the repeats on that chromosome alone.

The View switch draws the same names as a Sankey diagram: bars joined by ribbons, where a ribbon's thickness is how much of the parent name goes into the child name.

Which one to count

Count and test subfamilies. A family is the sum of its subfamilies, and a class is the sum of its families, so every read sits in all three at once. Use family and class to group results, never as extra rows in the same sum or test.
