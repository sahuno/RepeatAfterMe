Counting tools

Every repeat-counting tool makes two choices: whether it reports one count per copy or one per subfamily, and how it splits a read that fits several places. Subfamily totals survive most choices. Copy counts depend on the reads that fit one copy alone.

Seven counts from the same reads

Six made-up copies: four of subfamily A and two of subfamily B. Each copy has a stretch of its own, a stretch it shares with its subfamily, and a stretch both subfamilies share. Tap a copy to switch it off, on, or twice as on. Each tick is one read; a read fits every copy that has the stretch it came from. Several rules use EM (expectation-maximization): a read that fits several copies is split by how busy each copy looks from the reads that fit it alone, and the split is repeated until it stops changing.

Tested on simulated reads

Each tool counted the same simulated reads, cut from the mouse (mm10) and human (hg38) genomes so that the true copy of every read is known: 2,000 perfect 100-base reads per subfamily, 2,322,000 reads from 1,161 mouse subfamilies and 2,474,000 from 1,237 human ones. STAR aligned them keeping up to 100 places per read, the TEtranscripts setting. The reads were cut two ways: evenly from every copy of each subfamily, and from three copies per subfamily with the rest silent.

  • Every rule except -M, which counts reads several times, leaves out at least 17% of young-subfamily reads in mouse and 15% in human when every copy is on. 18.0% of those mouse reads and 12.5% of the human ones never aligned, so no counting rule can recover them (One read, many places).
  • With every copy on, -M --fraction puts the fewest reads on a wrong copy among the rules that count at least 70% of young-subfamily reads: 9.2% in mouse, against 11.9% for TElocal and 15.8% for Telescope's default, and 4.2% in human, against 7.9% and 10.5%.
  • With three copies on, the copy-level tools find them. Telescope's default puts 0.5% of young-subfamily reads on a wrong copy in mouse and 1.9% in human, and TElocal 5.1% and 6.5%, against 27.8% and 15.2% for -M --fraction.
  • Subfamily totals barely depend on the rule once multi-mapping reads are shared out: TEcount and -M --fraction count 95.6% to 99.9% of middle and old subfamily reads, in both species and both versions.
  • TElocal writes each copy's count as a whole number and drops the fraction (int(val) in its output code), so reads spread thinly over many copies vanish. With every copy on it counts 95.8% of old-subfamily reads in mouse and 95.7% in human, against 99.4% and 99.7% for -M --fraction.
  • Telescope's confident-only column puts 2.0% of reads or fewer on a wrong copy in every case, but leaves out up to 45.3% of young-subfamily reads in mouse and 32.0% in human.

Limits: perfect single-end reads with no sequencing errors and no strain or population differences; every tool on its default settings (Telescope's EM stopped at its 100-round limit); the tools saw repeat annotations only, no genes. One copy is one RepeatMasker row, which can be a fragment of one insertion (Body and ends). "On a wrong copy" is a lower bound worked out from per-copy counts. SQuIRE was not run, because it insists on its own STAR alignment, and neither was SalmonTE, whose Repbase names do not line up with RepeatMasker's.

Which to pick

  1. To ask whether a subfamily goes up or down, use a subfamily count that shares out multi-mapping reads: TEcount in multi mode, or featureCounts -M --fraction with gene_id set to the subfamily. In the model, the EM rules keep both subfamily totals within half a read of the truth in every setting. -M --fraction gives part of the shared stretch to a silent subfamily: switch on all of A and it counts 6.7 reads for B, which made none.
  2. To ask which copy is expressed, use a copy-level tool (TElocal, SQuIRE or Telescope), and check how many reads fit each reported copy alone. The test on simulated reads above shows these tools finding three switched-on copies. Set the own stretch to 0% in the model: copy EM then spreads a subfamily's reads evenly over its copies, and Telescope's default drops them. Telescope's default also drops shared reads when several copies are equally busy: switch on all of A and it keeps 24 of 120 reads.
  3. Without a genome alignment, SalmonTE gives one count per reference sequence. It cannot say which copy, and its names have to be matched to RepeatMasker names by hand.
  4. For young subfamilies, leave out unique-only counts and featureCounts -M without --fraction. The first drops most of their reads, and the second counts one read once for every place it fits.
